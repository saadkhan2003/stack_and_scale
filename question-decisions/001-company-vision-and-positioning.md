# Question 001 — Company vision and positioning

**Status:** Locked  
**Implementation:** Phase 0 and Phase 1  
**Source:** Stack & Scale 100-question architecture interview

> The original conversational wording has been normalized for clarity. This record preserves the final decision after later refinements.

## Question

What does Stack & Scale sell now, and what should it be known for in three years?

## Your chosen direction

Operate as a product-and-services software company and become known as a highly trusted provider of dependable modern software.

## Why this was chosen

This reflects both sides of the business: repeatable products such as POS and tailor management, and higher-value custom engineering.

## Why it is a strong fit

- Creates a clear long-term identity
- Supports products and custom engagements
- Makes trust and real work central to the brand

## Tradeoffs and safeguards

A broad promise requires strong proof; the company must avoid unsupported superlatives.

## Decision rule

This decision remains valid unless real implementation evidence, a legal requirement, or a material business-model change justifies revisiting it. Any revision should be recorded as a new architecture decision rather than silently changing this file.

#### Explanation or resolution

Good — this already tells me something important: your website should **not look like a freelancer portfolio or a generic outsourcing agency**.

You effectively have **two business engines**:

1. **Software products / ready business systems**
   - POS systems
   - tailor-management systems
   - other local-business software
   - potentially inventory, accounting, CRM, restaurant, school, clinic, etc.

2. **Custom technology services**
   - Flutter/mobile development
   - AI agents
   - automation
   - data analysis
   - custom software
   - other engineering services

And you already have completed projects that can become proof.

Your 3-year ambition is also useful, but I would sharpen “best software provider ever” into something the website can actually communicate:

> **A highly trusted software engineering company that builds reliable, modern software products and custom digital systems for businesses — from local Pakistani businesses to larger international clients.**

The important word here is **trusted**. We can make the whole website reinforce that through actual projects, measurable results, technical depth, transparent processes, strong product demonstrations, testimonials, client logos, case studies, reliability/performance data, and polished UX—not just claims like “we transform businesses.”

I also see a potential differentiator: most Pakistani software-house websites heavily advertise “our services.” Yours could feel more like a **technology company that actually builds things**.

So rather than:

> Web Development
> App Development
> AI Solutions
> Contact Us

I am imagining something closer to:

**We build software businesses actually run on.**

Then immediately showing real products and systems:

`POS Platform` → live product preview
`Tailor Management` → workflow demonstration
`AI Agent` → interactive demonstration
`Mobile Application` → device showcase
`Analytics Platform` → real dashboard/case study

Services exist, but **the work leads the website**.

That would make it feel substantially more mature.
